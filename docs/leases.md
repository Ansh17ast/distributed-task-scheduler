# Worker Leases, Fencing & Failover Protocol

This document details the worker lease lifecycle, fencing token mechanics, process-local monotonic clock rules, and the clock-independent leader failover reconciliation policy.

---

## 1. Process-Local Monotonic Clock Rules

### Why Cross-Process Monotonic Comparison is Forbidden
In Go, `time.Now()` includes a monotonic component that measures elapsed nanoseconds relative to local process startup.
- **Critical Rule**: **A monotonic timestamp generated by Process A has zero meaning inside Process B.**
- Serializing a Go monotonic reading across Protobuf, JSON, or a Raft WAL and attempting to compare it against `time.Now()` on another node causes severe undefined behavior (negative intervals, massive clock leaps).
- **Our Clock Independence Architecture**:
  1. **Raft-Replicated State** stores strictly **logical lease information**:
     - `TaskID` (string)
     - `WorkerID` (string)
     - `LeaseEpoch` (uint64)
     - `LeaseDuration` (duration, e.g. 10s, NOT absolute timestamps)
     - `GrantedAtRaftIndex` / `GrantedAtRaftTerm`
  2. **Every Coordinator and Worker** uses its **own local monotonic clock** exclusively for measuring local elapsed durations.
  3. **Zero NTP / Wall-Clock Synchronization Dependency**: Failover correctness, lease expiration, and fencing do **not** rely on synchronized wall clocks across nodes.

## 3. Heartbeat Architecture & Raft Load

### Leader-Local In-Memory Heartbeats
To prevent saturating the Raft consensus log with high-frequency heartbeats, **worker heartbeats are NOT appended to the Raft log.**
- With 1,000 workers heartbeating every 3 seconds, logging heartbeats would impose ~333 Raft disk fsyncs per second.
- **Our Design**:
  - Heartbeats are tracked **locally in memory on the current leader**.
  - When the leader receives a heartbeat, it resets the worker's local deadline using its **own local monotonic clock**:
    $$\text{localDeadline} = \text{leaderLocalMonotonicNow} + T_{\text{lease}}$$
  - Raft log consensus is invoked **only for structural state mutations**: `CmdGrantTaskLease`, `CmdMarkTaskRunning`, `CmdReclaimTask`, `CmdCompleteTask`, `CmdFailTask`, `CmdRegisterWorker`, and `CmdExpireWorker`.

## 4. Fencing Tokens: The `LeaseEpoch` Mechanism

Every task maintains a 64-bit monotonically increasing integer `LeaseEpoch`.
1. Initialized to `0` when task is admitted.
2. Incremented atomically upon every lease grant (`CmdGrantTaskLease`):
   $$\text{Task}.\text{LeaseEpoch} \leftarrow \text{Task}.\text{LeaseEpoch} + 1$$
3. Committed to the Raft log.
4. Worker receives `LeaseEpoch` in its task assignment payload.
5. All worker RPCs (`Heartbeat`, `AckRunning`, `ReportSuccess`, `ReportFailure`) must carry the exact `LeaseEpoch`.
6. **Coordinator Validation Rule**:
   $$\text{if } \text{Incoming}.\text{LeaseEpoch} \neq \text{Task}.\text{LeaseEpoch} \implies \text{REJECT with } \texttt{ErrStaleLeaseEpoch}$$

## 5. Edge Case Analysis: Late, Stale, and Resuming Workers

### Case 1: Worker Finished Before Lease Expiry, but Completion RPC Delayed
- **Scenario**: Worker $W_1$ completes task execution at $t = 8\text{s}$ (lease expires at $10\text{s} + 2\text{s} = 12\text{s}$). Due to network congestion, the `ReportSuccess` packet arrives at the coordinator at $t = 13\text{s}$.
- **Resolution**:
  - At $t = 12\text{s}$, the coordinator lease reaper detected timeout and committed `CmdReclaimTask`, incrementing the task's epoch from $1 \to 2$ and moving the task to `LOST` $\to$ `READY`.
  - When $W_1$'s delayed `ReportSuccess(Epoch=1)` arrives at $t = 13\text{s}$, the coordinator sees $\text{Incoming}(1) < \text{Current}(2)$.
  - The delayed completion is **rejected** with `ErrStaleLeaseEpoch`.
  - **Safety**: Prevents a late-arriving completion from overwriting a new worker's active execution. Because execution is at-least-once, downstream side effects rely on idempotency keys.

### Case 2: Duplicate Completion RPC (Network Retry)
- **Scenario**: Worker finishes, sends `ReportSuccess`, and coordinator successfully commits `SUCCEEDED`. However, the coordinator's TCP ACK drops. Worker retries `ReportSuccess`.
- **Resolution**:
  - Coordinator inspects task state. Task is already `SUCCEEDED` with matching `LeaseEpoch`.
  - Coordinator executes an **idempotent no-op**, acknowledges success to the worker, and does not re-trigger downstream DAG promotions.

### Case 3: Worker Resumes After Being Considered Dead (Zombie Worker)
- **Scenario**: Worker $W_1$ suffers a 15-second kernel/GC freeze. Coordinator reclaims task and reassigns it to Worker $W_2$ under `LeaseEpoch = 2`. $W_1$ unfreezes and attempts to resume.
- **Resolution**:
  - Two layers of defense:
    1. **Worker-Side Preemption**: $W_1$'s local runner enforces a preemption deadline on its own monotonic clock at $t_{\text{preempt}} = T_{\text{lease}} - 1.5\text{s} = 8.5\text{s}$. If the worker runtime paused completely, when it unfreezes, `time.Now().After(deadline)` is immediately true, triggering context cancellation and killing local subprocesses.
    2. **Coordinator Fencing**: If $W_1$ attempts to report status anyway, its `LeaseEpoch = 1` is rejected with `ErrStaleLeaseEpoch`.

### Case 4: Worker Reconnects With an Old Lease
- **Scenario**: Worker $W_1$ reboots after a crash and reconnects to the coordinator, sending a heartbeat claiming it still holds task $T_1$ with `LeaseEpoch = 1`.
- **Resolution**:
  - Coordinator checks active lease map: $T_1$ has already been reclaimed and assigned to $W_2$ with `LeaseEpoch = 2`.
  - Coordinator response flags $T_1$ in `revoked_tasks`.
  - $W_1$ tears down any lingering local execution state, cleans scratch files, and marks its slot as free.

## 6. Leader Failover Lease Reconciliation Policy (Clock-Independent)

When Coordinator 1 (Leader) crashes and Coordinator 2 is elected New Leader, Coordinator 2 **cannot and does not compare timestamps created by Coordinator 1.**

### The Failover Reconciliation Mechanism
1. **Reconstruct Committed Logical Lease State**:
   - Coordinator 2 reads the FSM for all tasks in `LEASED` and `RUNNING` states.
   - For each active task, the committed FSM provides: `(TaskID, AssignedWorkerID, LeaseEpoch, LeaseDuration)`.
2. **Start Local Reconciliation Timer**:
   - Coordinator 2 initializes an in-memory reconciliation window using its **own local monotonic clock**:
     $$t_{\text{reconcile\_deadline}} = t_{\text{coord2\_local\_monotonic\_now}} + T_{\text{reconcile}} \quad (\text{baseline } T_{\text{reconcile}} = 4\text{s})$$
   - During this window, Coordinator 2 opens its worker gRPC endpoints to accept worker heartbeats.
3. **Worker Reconnect & Lease Adoption**:
   - Active workers detect the TCP disconnect from Coordinator 1, back off, and connect to Coordinator 2.
   - The worker sends a heartbeat containing `(TaskID, WorkerID, LeaseEpoch)`.
   - When Coordinator 2 receives this heartbeat with matching `(WorkerID, LeaseEpoch)`:
     Coordinator 2 adopts the lease into its local in-memory tracker, establishing a new deadline on its own monotonic clock:
     $$\text{localDeadline} = t_{\text{coord2\_local\_monotonic\_now}} + T_{\text{lease}}$$
     The worker continues execution without disruption.
4. **Lease Reclamation (If Worker Dead)**:
   - If Coordinator 2's local monotonic clock reaches $t_{\text{reconcile\_deadline}}$ and no heartbeat has been received for that `(TaskID, LeaseEpoch)`:
     Coordinator 2 concludes the worker died before or during the failover.
     Coordinator 2 proposes `CmdReclaimTask` to Raft, moving the task to `LOST` $\to$ `RECOVERING` and re-enqueueing it.

### Assumptions and Limitations
- **Assumption**: A living worker can detect leader failure and establish a gRPC connection to the new leader within $T_{\text{reconcile}}$ (default $4\text{s}$, giving multiple retry attempts).
- **Safety Boundary**: If a healthy worker experiences extreme network latency $> T_{\text{reconcile}}$ during failover, Coordinator 2 will safely reclaim the task and increment `LeaseEpoch`. When the late worker eventually arrives, it is safely fenced out by `ErrStaleLeaseEpoch`.
- **Clock Independence**: Absolutely zero cross-node clock synchronization is required.
